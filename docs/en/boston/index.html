History

Inspired by the marathon at the 1896 Athens Olympics, the Boston Athletic Association (B.A.A.) held the first race in 1897 with just 15 starters. It is the world’s oldest annual marathon.

The race is traditionally held on Patriots’ Day in Massachusetts, the third Monday in April. Women were officially allowed to enter from 1972. Boston is known for its qualifying times (runners call it a “BQ”): most runners must first run a set time at another marathon to get in.

Boston

Boston is one of the oldest cities in the United States, a key stage of the American Revolution and an academic centre home to Harvard and MIT.

The course passes through eight cities and towns, from small suburbs into downtown. Mid-April weather is unpredictable: the race has seen both heat waves and cold rain with headwinds.

Course & elevation

Start 137 mHigh 137 mLow 3 mElevation gain ≈143 mElevation loss ≈277 m

The start in Hopkinton is at about 137 m and the finish at about 3 m, a net drop of more than 130 m. The long early descent punishes the quads, and the Newton Hills between 25 and 34 km climb one after another, the last one being Heartbreak Hill.

Updated November 2026

  1. 1897
    MomentThe first race

    John J. McDermott wins among 15 starters. It is the world’s oldest annual marathon.

  2. 1966
    MomentThe first woman to finish

    Refused an entry, Bobbi Gibb hides in bushes near the start, joins the field and finishes in about 3 hours 21 minutes.

  3. 1967
    MomentSwitzer’s race bib

    Kathrine Switzer enters as “K. V. Switzer” and gets a bib. An official tries to rip it off during the race, and the photos become a symbol for women’s distance running. The women’s race becomes official in 1972.

  4. 1975
    MomentThe wheelchair division begins

    Bob Hall finishes in a wheelchair, making Boston one of the first marathons to welcome wheelchair athletes.

  5. 1980
    MomentRosie Ruiz cheats

    The first woman across the line, Rosie Ruiz, turns out to have jumped into the race less than a mile from the finish. Her title is stripped and given to Jacqueline Gareau.

  6. 2013
    MomentFinish-line bombing

    On 15 April two bombs explode near the finish, killing 3 people and injuring hundreds. “Boston Strong” becomes the city’s rallying cry.

  7. 2014
    MomentMeb wins for the US

    A year after the bombing, Meb Keflezighi wins the men’s race, the first American man to win Boston since 1983.

  8. 2018
    MomentDes Linden in the cold rain

    In freezing rain and strong winds, Des Linden becomes the first American woman to win since 1985.

  9. 2020
    MomentCancelled for the first time

    The pandemic turns the race into a virtual event. In 2021 it moves to October.

  10. 2026
    RecordKorir runs 2:01:52

    At the 130th race, John Korir wins in 2:01:52, breaking the 2:03:02 Geoffrey Mutai had held since 2011. Sharon Lokedi wins the women’s race for the second year running.
