History

In 1970 Fred Lebow and Vince Chiappetta held the first race as laps of Central Park: 127 starters, 55 finishers and a one-dollar entry fee.

In 1976, to mark the US Bicentennial, the course was expanded to run through all five boroughs, which is the format it keeps today. New York now has more finishers than any other marathon in the world.

New York

New York is made up of five boroughs: Manhattan, Brooklyn, Queens, the Bronx and Staten Island. It is the largest city in the United States and a global centre of finance and culture.

The race crosses five bridges, and every borough has its own neighbourhoods and style of cheering. Early-November mornings are about 5–12°C, and the start villages are often very cold.

    MomentThe first race, in Central Park

    127 starters and 55 finishers; Gary Muhrcke wins and the entry fee is one dollar.

    MomentThrough all five boroughs

    To celebrate the US Bicentennial, the course expands to today’s five-borough route.

  3. 1978
    RecordGrete Waitz’s first win

    Norway’s Grete Waitz wins her debut in a world record and goes on to win New York nine times.

  4. 2009
    MomentMeb wins for the US

    Meb Keflezighi becomes the first American man to win New York since 1982.

  5. 2012
    MomentCancelled by Hurricane Sandy

    After the hurricane devastates the city, the race is called off two days before the start.

  6. 2017
    MomentFlanagan’s victory

    Shalane Flanagan becomes the first American woman to win New York since 1977.

  7. 2023

  8. 2025
