History

The modern Chicago Marathon began in 1977 as the Mayor Daley Marathon, with about 4,000 runners. Financial trouble interrupted the race in the 1980s before it got back on its feet.

Today Chicago is known for being flat, big and well supported. The course passes through 29 neighbourhoods and has produced both men’s and women’s world records.

Chicago

Chicago sits on the south-western shore of Lake Michigan and is the third-largest city in the United States. Rebuilt after the Great Fire of 1871, it gave the world its first skyscrapers and is still a pilgrimage site for architecture lovers.

Early-October mornings are about 8–15°C, but lakefront weather changes quickly, and heat has stopped the race early before.

Course & elevation

The elevation only varies between 177 and 184 m, so the course is essentially flat. The only noticeable rise is the Roosevelt Road bridge in the last 400 m, jokingly called “Mount Roosevelt”.

  1. 1977
    MomentFirst Mayor Daley Marathon

    About 4,000 runners take part, beginning the modern Chicago Marathon.

  2. 1985
    RecordJones and Benoit Samuelson

    Steve Jones runs 2:07:13, one second off the world record, and Joan Benoit Samuelson sets an American record of 2:21:21.

  3. 1999
    RecordKhannouchi 2:05:42

    Khalid Khannouchi breaks the men’s world record in Chicago.

  4. 2002
    RecordRadcliffe 2:17:18

    Paula Radcliffe breaks the women’s world record in Chicago and lowers it again in London the following year.

  5. 2007
    MomentRace stopped by heat

    In unusually hot weather, organisers halt the race about three and a half hours after the start.

  6. 2019
    RecordKosgei 2:14:04

    Brigid Kosgei breaks the women’s world record Radcliffe had held for 16 years.

  7. 2023
    RecordKiptum 2:00:35

    Kelvin Kiptum sets the men’s world record. He died in a car crash in February 2024, aged 24.

  8. 2024
    RecordChepngetich 2:09:56

    Ruth Chepngetich becomes the first woman under 2 hours 10 minutes. She received a three-year ban for an anti-doping violation in 2025, and the time is still listed as the world record.

  9. 2026
    MomentKipruto 2:01:09

    Amos Kipruto wins in the second-fastest time in Chicago history, and Hawi Feysa wins the women’s race for the second year running.
